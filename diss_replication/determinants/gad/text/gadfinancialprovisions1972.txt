                  REPORT BY THE GOVERNMENT ACTUARY ON
                   THE FINANCIAL PROVISIONS OF THE BELL
                        RELATING TO GREAT BRITAIN

    To the Right Hon. SIR KEITH JOSEPH, M.P.,
    Secretary of State for Social Services

    Sir,

      I submit the following report on the financial provisions of the Social
    Security Bill 1972 in so far as they relate to Great Britain.

      1. The Bill gives effect to the proposals for the future development of State
    and occupational pension provision and for changes in the structure of the
    national insurance and industrial injuries schemes set out in the White Paper
    entitled, “Strategy for Pensions” (Cmnd. 4755) published in September 1971.
    The provisions of the Bill, which applies to Northern Ireland as well as Great
    Britain, are described in detail in an explanatory memorandum (Cmnd. 5142)
    and the principal changes are summarised below. The target date for the
    implementation of the proposals is April 1975 and this date has been assumed
    for the purpose of the estimates in this report.

      2. Following the implementation of the proposals there will be a basic
    scheme which will be compulsory for employed and self-employed persons and
    will continue to provide national insurance benefits on the present pattern.
    As now, there will be separate schemes for Great Britain and Northern Ireland
    (with reciprocal arrangements to ensure that contribution and benefit rates
    will be the same in the two countries) but for each there will be only one fund
    combining the present National Insurance and Industrial Injuries Funds.
    The present report presents estimates of the future income and outgo of the
    combined National Insurance Fund in Great Britain and there will be a separate
    report on the Northern Ireland Fund.

       3. In addition to the basic scheme the Bill makes provision for a reserve
     pension scheme which will be compulsory for employees not covered for at
     least a prescribed minimum level of pension and widows’ benefits under an
     occupational scheme. There will be one reserve pension scheme for the whole
     of the United Kingdom.

     on all earnings up to an upper limit of £48 a week. Certain office-holders with
     emoluments chargeable to income tax under Schedule E (PAYE) who are
     treated as self-employed under the present scheme will in future be liable

       11. It is proposed that, corresponding to the earnings-related contributions
    for employed earners, Class 4 contributions should be payable by self-employed
    earners on profits or gains from a trade, profession or vocation chargeable to
    income tax under Case 1 or Case II of Schedule D. These Class 4 contributions
    will normally be in addition to the flat-rate contributions mentioned above and
    will be assessed at a rate of 5 % on the part of the profits or gains in a year
    between £1,150 and £2,500.

    Benefits
       12. The flat-rate benefits under the present national insurance and industrial
    injuries schemes will remain unchanged but the conditions for entitlement to
    benefit will be amended to adapt them to the new system of contributions.
    The formula for calculating earnings-related supplements to unemployment
    and sickness benefit and maternity and widow’s allowance will be modified so
    that the lower and upper limits of the range of earnings on which benefit is
    calculated will coincide respectively with the lower and upper earnings limits
    for the purpose of Class 1 contributions in the relevant tax year.

       13. The reduced rates of flat-rate unemployment and sickness benefits for
    juveniles will be abolished and persons under 18 will be eligible for the same
    benefits, including earnings-related supplements, as other earners.

        14. For persons who defer retirement beyond the minimum pension age
     increments to pension will be awarded on the basis of the number of weeks of
     benefit forgone and effectively will be at a higher rate than at present. Also it is
     proposed that increments, including those earned before the start of the new
     arrangements, should be included in the annual reviews of benefit rates (see
     paragraph 18).

        15. The new earnings-related contributions will not give title to any earnings-
     related pensions but existing rights to graduated pension secured by the payment
     of graduated contributions under the present scheme will be preserved.

     National Insurance Fund
        16. It is proposed that the assets of the Industrial Injuries Fund and the
     National Insurance (Reserve) Fund should be transferred to the National
     Insurance Fund, to give a single fund to which all contributions, after deduction
     of the parts relating to the National Health Service and the Redundancy Fund,
     would be credited and from which benefits under the basic scheme and under
     the Industrial Injuries Acts would be payable, with the exception as now of
     old persons’ pensions under the National ln.surance Acts 1970 and 1971, to be
     known in future as Category C and D retirement pensions, and of attendance
     allowance. These will continue to be met from the Consolidated Fund.

     Treasury supplement
        17. The Bill provides for a Treasury supplement to the National Insurance
     Fund of 18% of all contributions credited to that Fund; this is approximately
     the same proportion as results under the present legislation.

     Annual reviews of benefits
        18. The Bill requires the Secretary of State each year to review the level of
     benefits under the basic scheme and the Industrial Injuries Acts having regard
     to changes which have taken place in the level of prices and earnings since the

                                   THE RESERVE PENSION SCHEME
              34. Under Part III of the Bill there will be a separate reserve pension scheme
           to provide earnings-related personal and widows’ pensions for employed
           earners who are not in recognised pensionable employment. The scheme
           will be administered by an independent Reserve Pension Board and will have
           one fund covering members in Northern Ireland as well as those in Great
           Britain. It will be financed by contributions from the members and their
           employers with no subvention from the Exchequer. The scheme will not be
           financed on the pay-as-you-go basis but will be fully funded, the contributions
           of each generation of contributors being invested until required to meet the
           corresponding payments of pension so that a substantial fund will be built up.

           Contributions
             35. Contributions will be payable from the beginning of the tax year following
           the 21st birthday up to the end of the tax year preceding the attainment of age
           65 for men (60 for women) and will be assessed on the same range of earnings
           as Class 1 contributions to the basic scheme. The rate of contribution will be
           4% of which 1J% will be payable by the employed earner and 2-^% by the
           employer (or corresponding person or authority in the case of office-holders).
           Married women and widows will be required to contribute at the same rate as
           other employed earners.

           Benefits
              36. The benefits will consist of pensions from age 65 for men or age 60 for
           women and widows’ pensions of one-half of the husband’s pension or, in the
           case of death before age 65, of one-half of the prospective pension secured by
           contributions paid up to the time of his death. The scheme will be operated on
           a profit-sharing basis and the benefits will arise in two parts, the pension
           secured through the payment of contributions in accordance with the scales
           laid down in the Bill and bonus additions arising from the periodic distributions
           of surpluses (see paragraph 39).


             37.               The membership of the scheme will consist of perso
           of both sexes with differing career patterns and will include many who will pay
           contributions for only relatively short periods as well as persons who remain
           within the scope of the scheme for most of their working lives. Moreover, the
           distribution of the membership may change significantly over the years as a
           result of changes in occupational pensions provision. In these circumstances it
           has been considered essential that the benefits should be such as to give value
           for money on an actuarial basis to each individual contributor and, accordingly,
           the scales of pensions will be based not only on the amount of contributions
           but also on the sex of the contributor and the age at the time the contributions
           are paid. The proposed scales are set out in Schedule 18 of the Bill and values
           for specimen ages are reproduced in Table 5.

           Membership and size of Fund
             43. Allowing for the age limits now proposed, there will be about 17J million
          employees in the United Kingdom at the start of the new scheme who will have
          to be covered for earnings-related pension either by recognised occupational
          schemes or by the reserve pension scheme. The number of members of
          occupational schemes in the United Kingdom is currently about 11 million
          which, however, includes some persons outside the age limits. Almost all
          occupational schemes will require some amendment in order to satisfy the
          recognition tests; in most cases the amendments would not be very costly but a
          minority of schemes would need major improvements. Probably most employers
          will wish to seek exemption from the reserve pension scheme if only modest
          changes are necessary for this purpose but it is less easy to forecast whether
          recognition will be sought where this would require major improvements. It is
          also difficult to assess to what extent employers may broaden their schemes to
          cover a wider range of employees, and how many new schemes may be set up—
          especially for manual workers. In view of these uncertainties any estimate of
          reserve pension scheme membership must be largely conjectural but it seems
          possible that it might initially be of the order of 7 million of whom perhaps
          one-half would be women.

                           APPENDIX : THE BASIS OF THE ESTIMATES
                                           THE BASIC SCHEME
            1. Subject to the changes referred to in the report, the proposed basic scheme
         represents a continuation of the existing national insurance and industrial injuries
         schemes and the estimates of future income and outgo have to a large extent been
         based on the experience under the present schemes.
            2. The National Insurance and Industrial Injuries Acts provide for the Government
         Actuary to review the operation of the schemes at five-yearly intervals. The require-
         ment for the reviews due in respect of the period ended 31st March 1969 was waived by
         Section 10(3) of the National Insurance Act, 1969, but a detailed description of the
         experience under the schemes over the years up to 1969 and of the assumptions made
         for the future if the present schemes were to continue is given in the Appendix of the
         Report by the Government Actuary on the Financial Provisions of the National
         Superannuation and Social Insurance Bill, 1969 (Cmnd. 4223). For the present esti-
         mates the assumptions for the 1969 report have been revi.sed in the light of the addi-
         tional data which have become available during the last three years.

                                        Future Population of Great Britain
            3. Estimates of both the income and the outgo of the National Insurance Fund in
         future years depend upon a projection of the total population of Great Britain, sub-
         divided by sex, age and marital condition. Earlier this year population projections
         were prepared based on estimates of the population at mid-year 1971 which took
         account of the preliminary results of the 1971 Census; these projections, including
         particulars of the underlying assumptions, are being published by the Office of Popula-
         tion Censuses and Surveys under the title “Population Projections 1971-2011”.
         The projection for Great Britain has been used for the estimates for the basic scheme
         in this report.

                                            Numbers of Contributors
           4. Apart from the special provisions for persons with low earnings, the coverage
         of the scheme and the basis of classification of earners into employed and self-employed
         categories will remain almost unchanged (despite some fundamental changes in the
         way the contribution liability will be assessed). It has, therefore, been possible to bai2
         estimates of the average numbers of contributors in future years upon data relating to
         the numbers of insured persons under the provisions at present in force.
            5. Estimates of the average number of contributors in each class in 1970 and 1971,
         subdivided by age and sex (and, for women, by marital condition) have been related
         to the corresponding numbers in the total population of Great Britain in order to
         obtain proportions insured. For this purpose the number of contributors has, as in the
         past, been taken as the average number within the scope of the scheme during each
         year, including cases in which contributions were excused because of unemployment
         or incapacity for work. The proportions insured were adjusted where appropriate to
         allow for the exclusion of those persons who would be within the scope of the present
         scheme but whose earnings are expected to be below the lower earnings limit for
         contribution liability under the proposed scheme. The effect of this has been to exclude
         nearly one million people, mainly married women working parl-tiiTie, young persons
         under 18 years of age and retirement pensioners.
           6. The proportions insured were further adjusted as explained in the following
         paragraphs to allow for expected changes in the proportions at work in the future and
         they were then applied to the estimates of the future population to obtain the numbers
         of persons within the scope of the basic scheme who will be liable to pay Class 1 or
         Class 2 contributions or who would have been liable but for unemployment or in-
         capacity for work.

         the proportions contributing under the national insurance scheme. Allowance has

                                                          Earnings
               15. The estimates in the report are on the basis of the level of earnings estimated for
           October 1972 when the increases in benefits under the National Insurance Act, 1972
           came into force. In making estimates of the yields of the earnings-related contributions
           it is not sufficient to have regard merely to the average earnings of all employed earners,
           because contributions are not payable on earnings above the upper limit of £48 a week
           (in current terms) or in respect of persons whose earnings in a week are less than £8.
           It has, therefore, been necessary to consider the distribution of the actual earnings of
           individuals in a week and, in view of the differing trends of the numbers of men, women,
           boys and girls, to do so for each of these four groups separately.

                                           Retirement Pensions
          19. Estimates of future numbers of retirement pensioners have been obtained from
       the numbers at ages over pension age in the population projections. It has been assumed
       that the numbers of men reaching age 65 in future years with title to pension will exceed
       the numbers in the home population by about 1.5%. The reason for the excess is that
       some pensions are paid to persons living abroad who have contributed under the
       national insurance scheme. For women allowance has been made for the varying
       proportion contributing according to marital condition. It has been assumed that in
       future about 90% of single and divorced women reaching pension age will be entitled
       to pension. For married women the proportion so entitled on their own insurance has
       been taken as 10% reducing to 8% by about 1980. Most other married women will be
       entitled to a pension on their husband’s insurance when he retires. For widows reaching
       pension age it has been assumed that the number entitled to benefit either by reason of
       their own or their late husband’s contributions will, as for men, exceed the numbers in
       the home population by 1.5 %, but this total includes some widows who, because they
       are receiving industrial injuries or war widows’ benefits, will not receive retirement
       pensions.

       Deferment of retirement
          20. The trend towards earlier retirement has continued with the proportion of men
       aged between 65 and 70 who are retired rising from 80% in 1968 to about 84% at the
       end of 1971. The rate of increase, however, was somewhat lower than that experienced
       in earlier years. For the 1969 report it was assumed that the proportion would rise to
       93 % by the late 1970’s and thereafter remain constant. In view of the apparent slowing
       down in the trend towards earlier retirement it has now been assumed that this ultimate
       level will not be reached until the early 1980’s,

         21. For women contributors there is also evidence of a trend towards earlier retire-
       ment and it has been assumed that this will continue until the proportion retired at
       ages 60 to 64 reaches 90% in the late 1970’s.

                                              Maternity Benefits
           41. On the basis of recent experience it has been assumed that maternity grants will

         for 18 weeks or more the average duration is about 17 weeks.

                                                  Death Grant
            42. It might be expected that death grant would be paid for virtually all deaths of
         persons who had not reached age 65 (men) or 60 (women) before 5th July 1948. It is
         found, however, that in practice no grant is paid in a significant proportion of cases and
         it has been assumed on the basis of recent experience that grants will be paid for 97 %
         of such male deaths and 93 % of such female deaths.

                                     RESERVE PENSION SCHEME
          57. The rate of interest assumed and the provision made for expenses in calculating
        the benefit scales under the reserve pension scheme are referred to in paragraph 38
        of the report. In addition assumptions had to be made about mortality and about
        factors such as the proportions married amongst male members of the scheme and the
        relative ages of husbands and wives.
           58. The mortality of members of the reserve pension scheme is likely to be lighter
        than that of the population as a whole since persons who are chronically sick and have
        high mortality rates will either be excluded altogether or will have built up compara-
        tively :;mall amounts of pension and will thus have little weight in the overall experience.
        The calculations were, therefore, based on the mortality rales assumed for the popula-
        tion projections (see paragraph 3), adjusted to make allowance for this factor on the
        basis of a comparison of the mortality of members of occupational pension schemes
        with that of the population as a whole. The rates assumed allow for future improve-
        ment in mortality in the lieht of past experience. Specimen values are shown in Table
        20.
           59. The mortality of wives and widows of reserve pension scheme members has been
        assumed to be the same as in the general population. The mortality of married women
        is lighter, and that of widows heavier, then the mortality of all women and this has been
        allowed for by reference to the 1960-62 mortality experience according to marital
        condition described in the Government Actuary’s report on the English Life Tables
        No. 12*.
          60. The proportion of male members who are married at each age were assumed
        to be the same as the estimated proportions for the population as a whole in the
        population projections. These allow for increases in the proportions married which
        are expected in the future, particularly at the younger ages. It was assumed that on
        average wives would be three years younger than their husbands. Allowance was also
        made for the fact that widows’ pensions will cease on remarriage on the basis of rates
