## Where Sam's reading needs care (flagged, not changed)

1. **The PSA passage (Sam agreed, 30 Sep).** Aim 5's PSA objective is a race-equality objective for the minority ethnic population's access to public
   services. The paper says it "has not so far explicitly recognised migrants as a separate group". It does not connect entry policy
   to the provision of public services. The entry aim is Aim 6. The stronger point for the thesis is on p. 17: the Home Office's
   immigration PSA targets with the Treasury were "process targets, and relate solely to asylum seekers". Read with p. 51 ("DfEE has
   no economic targets or objectives for OLS"), this means that in 2000 neither department carried any published economic or fiscal
   target for migration. The paper then asks for Aim 6 to be made "more operational".
2. **"Current" unemployment in the regression.** Footnote 13 gives "the level of UK unemployment (ILO basis)" without a timing. It also
   says "Dependent variables", which is a slip for explanatory variables. The outcome is net migration of non-UK, non-EU nationals in
   levels. For the "prediction" the authors hold unemployment constant and stress it is "not a forecast".
3. **The White Paper does not announce the transfer.** It reports it as already done: "Work Permits (UK), now part of the Immigration
   and Nationality Directorate" (p. 40, February 2002). Sam (30 Sep): the White Paper is cited to document the history and the change of
   authority, not to date a structural break. Dating: the Prime Minister's machinery-of-government announcement of 8 June 2001 (the day after the general election) moved the
   Overseas Labour Service (Sheffield) to the Home Office as Work Permits (UK). CORRECTION (30 Sep, from Sam's upload of the Order):
   the Transfer of Functions (Miscellaneous) Order 2001, SI 2001/3500, does NOT cover work permits. It was made 31 October 2001, laid
   5 November 2001, and came into force 26 November 2001. It moves functions OUT of the Home Office: to the Lord Chancellor (data
   protection, human rights, freedom of information, Monarchy, Channel Islands and Isle of Man, marriage); to DCMS (gambling,
   horse-racing, liquor licensing); to DTI (street trading); to DTLR (fire, elections); and to DEFRA (animals). Its "immediately
   before 7th June 2001" clause refers to functions then held by the Home Secretary. The earlier note, taken from a search summary,
   was wrong. The same June 2001 reorganisation that took these functions out of the Home Office brought work permits in. The work
   permit move itself may not have needed an Order, because immigration statutes confer powers on "the Secretary of State"
   generically. That is a possibility, not verified. Sources to check: the Home Office departmental report 2002, the Work Permits
   (UK) annual report, or Hansard for June–July 2001. The ministerial written answers mentioned by Gemini (Angela Eagle, Beverley
   Hughes) have NOT been verified; check Hansard before citing them.
4. **Indirect Home Office control before the transfer (Sam, 30 Sep).** Supported. Entry clearance was run by the Home Office/FCO Joint
   Entry Clearance Unit, and IND held immigration control (p. 19, 5.2). The same page on the other side: "the immigration
   authorities generally accept a work permit as evidence for a decision to admit" (p. 20, 5.6). So the Home Office held the gate,
   but in practice it admitted permit holders.
5. **The support ratio.** The figures are the UN zero-migration projection, used in the paper's discussion of the UN (2000)
   *Replacement Migration* report and its relevance to Europe and the UK. Sam (30 Sep): citing a new and contentious UN paper once
   does not diminish the documented role of the GAD projections; accepted. Minor: the text says 16–64 and 65+, while the UN figures
   are labelled 15–64 and 65+.
6. **The fiscal estimate is annual, not lifetime.** The £2.6bn (p. 44, 6.50) is a one-year cross-section for 1998/99: revenue from
   the foreign-born minus spending on them in that year. It is the short-term fiscal benefit Sam emphasises. The lifetime figure in
   the same paragraph ($80,000 per migrant) is from a US study.
7. **p. 12 regression (Sam, 30 Sep).** The timing of unemployment could go either way. The regression uses the ILO measure of
   unemployment.

## How it bears on the econometrics (regime_timing.R, 30 Sep 2026)

- A Home Office research unit, in 2001, used a zero-migration projection of the dependency ratio to argue that migration is a
  "desirable complement" to other responses to ageing, and estimated the fiscal contribution of the foreign-born (+£2.6bn,
  about 1p on the basic rate). This is contemporaneous documentary evidence that the projection object in the model was in use
  inside government, and that migration was assessed in fiscal terms. It is not evidence that the Treasury set migration.
- The paper's own model uses the same building blocks as ours: lagged migration, the ILO unemployment level, and flows in levels
  on a citizenship basis.
- The 2001 transfer of Work Permits (UK) shows no break in the projection effect. The break comes in 2008–10. That window contains
  the replacement of work permits by the Points-Based System (Tier 2, November 2008), the financial crisis, and the 2010
  net-migration target. Annual data cannot separate these three.
